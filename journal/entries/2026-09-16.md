# 2026-09-16

## What happened

Miguel described a gap: he can gather technical knowledge quickly and reproduce it, but
struggles to explain the underlying mechanism, reasoning, or tradeoffs when asked directly.
This shows up in interviews. He suspects it bleeds into other parts of his life too.

## Separating the categories

- What he knows: he can implement and reproduce technical solutions.
- What he's assuming: that this means he doesn't "actually understand" the material, and that
  the cause is laziness.
- What remains unknown: which of the following actually describes what's happening —
  procedural-only understanding, weak retrieval, disconnected pieces never assembled into a
  model, learning by imitation rather than reconstruction, or just limited practice explaining
  things out loud. Possibly some combination.

## Investigation opened

A concrete test was proposed: pick something he believes he knows well (Redis caching,
database indexes, HTTP, Spring dependency injection), and without looking anything up,
answer: what is it, why does it exist, how does it work, what would happen if it were removed.
Then examine exactly where the explanation breaks. This gives evidence instead of a label.

## Carrying forward

- What happened: a pattern surfaced between "knowing" and "being able to explain."
- What I learned: not yet concluded. This is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- What remains uncertain: whether "lazy" describes the behavior accurately, or whether it's a
  more precise gap (retrieval, connection, or practice) that got the wrong label.
- What I want to do next: run the retrieval test on a real technical topic and record where
  the explanation actually breaks down.
