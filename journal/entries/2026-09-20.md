A set of principles was brought in to find genuine use for over the course of this learning
work, not applied yet to anything specific, recorded here as stated:

- Parkinson's law: work expands to fill the time given it; shrink the deadline, shrink the
  task.
- Hofstadter's law: everything takes longer than expected, even accounting for Hofstadter's
  law.
- Hanlon's razor: never attribute to malice what is adequately explained by carelessness or
  stupidity.
- The Pareto principle: roughly 80 percent of results come from 20 percent of effort.
- The Peter principle: people get promoted until they reach a job they're bad at, then stay
  there.
- Hick's law: more options mean slower decisions, cut the menu, decide faster.
- Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure.
- The Dunning-Kruger effect: the less you know, the more you overestimate how much you know.
- Occam's razor: the simplest explanation is usually the right one.
- Chesterton's fence: don't tear something down until you understand why it was built.
- Brooks's law: adding people to a late project makes it later, coordination cost is
  underestimated.

No connections drawn yet between these and the work already done. Two occurred while writing
this down, worth naming honestly as noticing, not as conclusions: Chesterton's fence is
close to the actual instruction already given for settlement-engine, understand it before
touching it. Goodhart's law is worth watching once Routine Machine's own completion rates
and streaks start accumulating real data, since the system exists to serve the goals behind
it, not to make its own numbers look good. Neither is claimed as established yet, both are
just noticed on first contact with the list.
