## The idea

A concept in another person's mind is not a reality. It is a picture they hold. They built it from what they saw, what they fear, and what they need to be true.

That picture becomes real in my life through one door: me. When I take it into my own head and treat it as true, it starts to steer what I do. If I do not take it in, it stays where it was made, in their head.

I am in control of that door.

## An example from work

A colleague says my work is slow. I can take that sentence in and repeat it to myself on every task. Within a week I rush, skip checks, and ship worse code. The sentence has made itself real through my own behaviour.

Or I can ask one question: is there a fact here I can act on? I look at my own record: how long my tickets take to close, compared with the team. If the record shows I am on pace, the sentence was a concept, and I drop it. If it shows a real delay, that is a fact, and I fix the cause. Either way I go back to my task list.

If the same colleague blocks my pull request, that is an action, and I deal with it as an action. Opinion and action are different things. I answer actions. I do not answer concepts.

## Ignorance as a choice

I mean ignorance in a narrow sense: deciding not to give attention to what others think of me. I do not deny facts. I do not ignore a deadline, a bug, or a rule.

I forget the opinions that are not mine to carry. That frees attention for what is mine: my work, my habits, my goals, the next small step.

## Why that is enough

Growth needs attention. Attention is limited. Every hour spent on what others hold about me is an hour not spent on what I can change. Spending it on my own work compounds. Spending it on their concepts returns nothing.

Nobody else can do my work for me, so focusing on what is mine is not a loss. It is the only place where progress happens.

## Where this stops

This does not mean other people never matter. Their actions, their decisions and their facts affect me, and I respond to those. It also does not mean feedback is noise. Feedback that points to a fact is useful. I check it against evidence.

The line is simple: take in what I can verify and act on. Leave the rest where it was made.
