## What I read

This is my own understanding of the chapter, not a checked account of what it says. Sapiens
suggests some of how we behave comes from hunter-gatherer minds living in a post-industrial
world. We're built to move and explore, but modern life is built around staying put and
accumulating things. The book links that mismatch to depression. I'm treating that link as an
argument, not a finding. Real research points to many causes, movement being one.

## The question it raised

That morning I'd exercised before reading, because I need to feel physically settled before
my mind works. Was that exercise the real thing, or one more modern substitute for foraging
and tracking?

## The answer I'm holding for now

Both. I felt off before I had any language for why. The reading gave me the language
afterward. And Routine Machine, with movement written into the schedule, was the answer I'd
already acted on before the insight existed. I'm holding that loosely. It will probably
widen as I read further.

## What I took from it

1. Action can come before understanding. The understanding still matters.
2. A persuasive chapter is an argument until I've checked it.
3. My summary of a book is mine, not the author's.
