## The line

"Peace does not come to the man who refuses to declare war on what is destroying him." I'd
seen it credited to Seneca's Letters to Lucilius. My own reading of it: forgiving bad habits,
feeding bad desires, and explaining yourself through them is how they end up in charge.

## The check

Before discussing it, I checked the attribution. It's a known case of misattribution. The
line circulates widely under Seneca's name, but it doesn't seem to appear anywhere in his letters.

## The idea anyway

A wrong author doesn't make an idea wrong, it just means I can't borrow his authority for it.
So I took it on its own terms. The part that holds up for me: an explanation I accept too
early starts reading all future evidence for me. Every new moment looks like confirmation.

## What I took from it

1. Check who said it before I quote it.
2. Keep the idea and the attribution separate.
3. An explanation accepted too early starts doing my thinking for me.
