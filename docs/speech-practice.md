# docs/speech-practice.md: speech practice plan

Started 2026-11-04. Goal: clearer, steadier speaking. Tools you named: randomwordgenerator.com, pen drills, tongue rotations, lip trills.

Note: I am not a speech therapist. These are common warm-ups and practice methods. I did not check studies for them, so treat any benefit as unproven for you. If you have a specific difficulty, a speech-language pathologist can assess it.

## Daily session, 15 minutes

1. Lip trills, 2 minutes. Relax your lips, blow air so they flutter, and glide your pitch up and down. If it will not flutter, press a finger lightly on each cheek. Drink water first. Stop if you feel lightheaded.
2. Tongue rotations, 1 minute. Circle your tongue slowly around the outside of your teeth behind closed lips, 3 to 5 slow rounds each direction. This warms up tongue movement. It is not proven to change clarity. Stop if your jaw or tongue hurts.
3. Pen drill, 5 minutes. The pen exercise exaggerates mouth movement. A reviewer flagged it as the riskiest item: a pen can cause choking or dental injury if you laugh, get startled or speak loudly. Use a clean pen with the cap on, hold it lightly and briefly while seated, and take it out between sentences. A safer option is to drop the pen and exaggerate your mouth movements instead. Read a page aloud slowly, then read it again without the pen.
4. Random word drill, 6 minutes. Open randomwordgenerator.com, take one word, and speak about it for 60 seconds using three parts: a point, a reason, an example. Repeat up to six times. Record at least the last two. Keep your pace slow.

On a skipped day, do only the lip trills and one word. Do not make up the missed time.

## What to measure

Once a week, record two 60-second talks: the same fixed prompt every week, and one random word. Track fillers per minute, pauses, and a clarity rating from 1 to 5. Ask a trusted listener to score one early clip and one recent clip without knowing which is which. Words per minute alone is not progress, because faster can be worse. Wait for six weeks of data before judging.

## Rules for the drills

- Slow beats fast. Clear first, speed later.
- Short sessions daily beat long sessions weekly.
- Stop any drill that causes pain or dizziness.
