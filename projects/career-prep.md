## Observed pattern (from journal, 2026-09-16)

Original hypothesis: a gap between knowing something and being able to explain it on demand,
showing up specifically in interviews. Framed as possibly a retrieval or reconstruction gap.

Tested against database indexing. Result: the explanation was complete and correctly
ordered, problem, mechanism, tradeoff, with no missing reasoning. The retrieval hypothesis
did not hold for this topic.

What surfaced instead: immediately after finishing a correct explanation, predicting the
audience was lost. The prediction wasn't supported by anything in the explanation itself.

Reframed using a distinction raised independently: not "does he know this well enough" but
"can he read the situation and choose the register it calls for." The indexing test suggests
the knowledge is there. What's untested is the second skill, and whether the anticipation of
losing an audience shows up as its own pattern outside technical explanations.

Status: possible signal shifting. Retrieval doesn't look like the core issue. Register
calibration and self-prediction of failure look more relevant. Needs more data before
naming either as an established pattern.
